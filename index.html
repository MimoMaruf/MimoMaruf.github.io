About me

I am an economist working on health economics, intergenerational mobility, public policy and development.

I use large household, patient and longitudinal datasets with applied microeconometric, causal and spatial methods to study how health, income and opportunity move across people, families and places. My aim is to produce careful evidence that is useful to governments and communities.

I am a Research Fellow at the Bangladesh Institute of Development Studies and a Casual Academic at the University of Technology Sydney and the University of Sydney. I completed my PhD in Economics at UTS in 2026. My thesis, Dimensions of Opportunity: Essays on Intergenerational Income and Health Mobility in Australia, examined the persistence of income and health outcomes across generations.

My research has involved Australian and international government agencies, universities and development organisations. I also teach economics, econometrics, statistics and policy evaluation.

Research

Health economics and equity

I study the economic burden of disease, out-of-pocket healthcare costs and the unequal financial consequences of illness. This work includes national and patient-level studies of tobacco use, dengue and healthcare spending in Bangladesh.

Intergenerational mobility

My doctoral research examines how income and health outcomes persist between parents and children, and how family environments and place shape opportunity. I work with HILDA, Australian family-linked data, cross-country income distributions and spatial information.

Applied public policy

I translate quantitative analysis into policy reports, briefings and evaluation findings for decision-makers. My experience includes program evaluation, distributional analysis, policy scenarios and stakeholder reporting.

Development, labour and climate

My broader work covers firm productivity and skills, poverty programs, labour markets, climate adaptation and vulnerable communities.

Teaching

I teach economics as an applied way of thinking: begin with a real question, make the assumptions visible, and then use evidence carefully. Since 2022 I have taught, tutored, demonstrated code and marked across undergraduate and postgraduate subjects.
